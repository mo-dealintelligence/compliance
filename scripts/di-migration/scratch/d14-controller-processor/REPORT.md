# D14 — Controller vs. processor: how Deal Intelligence should frame its role (GDPR + CCPA/CPRA)

Research date: 2026-10-31. Sources are public web pages fetched on that date; quotes are capped at 15 words per source. This is research, not legal advice; the DPA and DSR procedure should be reviewed by counsel before publication.

## 1. Recommendation

Deal Intelligence (DI) is **both**, split by data flow, and the DPA should say so explicitly rather than pick one label. For the data DI compiles and licenses (verified vendor-customer lists, named buyers, "competitor activity" events, contact details — call it **"DI Data"**), DI is an **independent controller** under GDPR and a **"business"** (and a registered **data broker**) under CCPA/CPRA; the customer is a **separate, independent controller** (GDPR) and a **"third party"** (CCPA) when it receives DI Data. For data the customer uploads for matching (account lists, contact lists, CRM/Slack sync — **"Customer Data"**), DI is a **processor** (GDPR) and **"service provider"** (CCPA) acting only on documented instructions. This two-flow structure is the clear market norm: Apollo, Lusha, Cognism, Clay, Clearbit/HubSpot Breeze, ZoomInfo and 6sense all publish it, and the EDPB says the same entity can be controller for some operations and processor for others (Guidelines 07/2020 paras 26, 82). The DPA should therefore be structured as **Part A — Processor Terms** (Art. 28(3) content + 11 CCR §7051 service-provider terms) covering Customer Data, and **Part B — Independent Controller Terms** (controller-to-controller, Module 1 SCCs/UK Addendum for any EU/UK export, 11 CCR §7053 third-party terms) covering DI Data, with a short **Part C** on CCPA roles (DI = business and data broker for DI Data; customer = third party; DI = service provider for Customer Data). Do **not** adopt ZoomInfo's "Submitted & Integrated" model (customer uploads flow back into the vendor dataset as C2C) unless DI actually wants to reuse uploads to enrich its database; by default, the processor terms must prohibit DI from combining Customer Data with DI Data for DI's own purposes (that prohibition is mandatory under §7051 and is what keeps DI a processor under EDPB para 81). The DSR procedure must have two tracks: (1) for DI Data, DI answers access/erasure/objection/opt-out requests itself as controller within one month (GDPR) / 45 days (CCPA), keeps a permanent suppression list, honors the absolute Art. 21(2) objection to direct marketing, pushes deletions to customers through the API/MCP delta feed, and checks the CPPA DROP list every 45 days as a registered data broker; (2) for Customer Data, DI does not answer the data subject substantively, forwards the request to the customer promptly (target 5 business days) and assists on instruction. The customer-facing text should also state that DI relies on **legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f))** for DI Data and gives Art. 14 notice, so customers can rely on DI's notice as part of their own LIA but remain independently responsible for their lawful basis when they use the data.

Pattern: 8 of 10 vendors publish a two-part DPA (processor for uploads, independent controller for the vendor's own dataset). None claims to be a pure processor for data it compiled itself; none claims joint controllership. The two that are vague publicly (Bombora, Demandbase) still state controller status for data they collect. Clay is the closest analogue to DI's "customer uploads a list, gets matched/enriched" flow and is the only one that expressly labels the customer a CCPA "third party" for vendor-supplied data.

## 3. Regulator guidance and enforcement

### EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 (v2.1, adopted 7 July 2021) — [PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)
- Role is factual, per processing activity: "the same entity may act at the same time as controller … and as processor" (para 26; repeated at 82).
- Contracts help but do not decide: a party "will be a controller even if a contract says that it is a processor" (para 29).
- Essential means (which data, how long, who gets it, whose data) are reserved to the controller (para 40). DI decides all of these for DI Data → DI is controller of DI Data.
- A processor that uses data for its own purposes becomes a controller for that processing and infringes GDPR (para 81, Art. 28(10)). This is why the processor terms must bar DI from folding uploads into DI Data.
- Transmitting a dataset between two entities "without jointly determined purposes or jointly determined means" = "transmission of data between separate controllers" (para 70). This is the vendor→customer DI Data flow: separate (independent) controllers, not joint.
- Art. 28(3) contract must cover DSR assistance; "the controller bears the responsibility for complying with such requests" (paras 130–132).

### UK ICO — [Controllers and processors guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/controllers-and-processors-a-guide/); [B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- Controllers "will be controllers regardless of how they are described in any contract"; a processor has no "purpose of your own for processing the data".
- Controllers processing the same data for different purposes are "not joint controllers".
- UK GDPR applies to named business contacts "even if they are acting in their business capacity"; legitimate interests is usually appropriate for B2B where PECR does not require consent; the Art. 21 objection to direct marketing is "absolute"; keep a suppression list rather than deleting.

### ICO data-broking enforcement (Experian) — [ICO 2020 investigation summary](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/ckgw3cesbqxeg0951zrt12490/ico-issues-results-of-investigation-into-data-broking-sector); [Upper Tribunal outcome](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/experians-win-against-the-ico-in-upper-tribunal/)
- ICO's 2020 findings against Experian/Equifax/TransUnion: opaque privacy notices, over-reliance on the "disproportionate effort" exemption from Art. 14 notices, LIAs that favoured commercial interests, and switching from consent to legitimate interests.
- First-tier Tribunal (20 Feb 2023) and Upper Tribunal (23 Apr 2024) largely sided with Experian: legitimate interests **can** support data broking/marketing profiling even where some find it "surprising"; a layered privacy notice was adequate; but the FTT still ordered Art. 14 notices to 5.3M people, and cost alone does not make notification disproportionate.
- Lesson for DI: the controller model is legally sound for a B2B broker, but only with a documented LIA, a real Art. 14 notice strategy, and a working objection/suppression process.

### France CNIL — B2B prospecting and reconstituted professional emails (16 Oct 2026) — [cnil.fr](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/reconstitution-adresses-professionnelles-quel-cadre); [summary](https://www.leto.legal/news/cnil-reconstitution-adresses-electroniques-professionnelles-interet-legitime-2026)
- Legitimate interest can cover B2B prospecting and even guessed professional addresses, "sous réserve de respecter certaines conditions"; it is "ni automatique ni systématique".
- Conditions: genuine interest in contacting the person; reasonable-expectation test (people with no identifiable online footprint or who shield contact details fail it); strict retention (drop the address if no effective contact in a reasonable time); any objection excludes the person immediately without justification; information duties apply.
- Relevance: DI's "confirmed to have accepted competitor outreach" signal is behavioural, not directory data; CNIL's reasonable-expectation framing is the test DI's LIA must pass.

### Belgium GBA — Recommendation 01/2025 on direct marketing (consultation 10 Mar 2025) — [PDF](https://autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications/aanbeveling-01-2025-over-de-verwerking-van-persoonsgegevens-bij-direct-marketing.pdf); Warning 15/2025 (24 Jan 2025) — [PDF](https://autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications/waarschuwing-nr.-15-2025.pdf)
- Data-broker activities are themselves direct-marketing activities (point 22); professional vs private address is not what decides whether a message is direct marketing (point 26); roles must be assessed factually per EDPB 07/2020 (point 36).
- Warning 15/2025: a company that used a lead-gen tool for B2B cold emails could not show its tool vendor complied (Arts 24, 28); the Chamber rejected legitimate interests for cold B2B email and required ePrivacy consent. Belgium is stricter than the UK/France on channel consent; it shows customers will ask DI for Art. 28 proof.

### Netherlands AP
No Dutch AP decision specific to B2B data brokers was found. The closest action is the AP's 2018 intervention over the Chamber of Commerce (KvK) selling trade-register data for direct marketing, which the AP said was "op het eerste gezicht slecht te verenigen met het oorspronkelijke doel" ([NOS, 17 Apr 2018](https://nos.nl/artikel/2227783-privacywaakhond-in-actie-tegen-datadelen-kamer-van-koophandel)); KvK later stopped selling address data. Treat the Netherlands as consent-leaning for electronic B2B marketing (like Belgium) when customers ask.

### CCPA/CPRA — definitions and data-broker status
- Civ. Code §1798.140 ([text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1798.140.&lawCode=CIV)): "business" (d) = for-profit entity meeting a threshold (one threshold is deriving 50% or more of annual revenue from selling/sharing PI, which a pure data vendor meets regardless of size — confirm against the current text); "service provider" (ag) = "a person that processes personal information on behalf of a business"; "third party" (ai) = "a person who is not any of the following" (the business, a service provider, a contractor); "sell" (ad) = "selling, renting, releasing, disclosing, disseminating, making available, transferring" PI "for monetary or other valuable consideration". Licensing DI Data for a fee is a "sale".
- Regulations: 11 CCR §7051 service-provider contracts must "Prohibit the service provider or contractor from selling or sharing personal information", bar use "outside the direct business relationship", and bar "combining or updating personal information" with other sources ([§7051](https://regulations.vlex.com/vid/cal-code-regs-tit-958213990)). 11 CCR §7053 third-party contracts must identify "the limited and specified purpose(s)", require the third party "to comply with all applicable sections of the CCPA", and give the business audit/stop-and-remediate rights ([§7053](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-7053)). A third party without such a contract "shall not collect, use, process, retain, sell, or share" the PI ([§7052](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-7052)).
- Delete Act, §1798.99.80 et seq. ([statute eff. 1 Jan 2026](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/data_broker_reg_delete_act_statute_eff_20260101.pdf)): data broker = "a business that knowingly collects and sells to third parties" PI of consumers "with whom the business does not have a direct relationship". DI fits this definition for DI Data. Register with the CPPA "On or before January 31 following each year" of activity; "Beginning August 1, 2026, a data broker shall access the accessible deletion mechanism" (DROP) at least every 45 days and delete within 45 days; audits from 1 Jan 2028; $200/day for non-registration and "$200 for each deletion request" per day unprocessed.
- Enforcement is live: CPPA fined ROR Partners $50,000 (Nov 2025) for failing to register as a data broker ([order](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/pdf/ror_partners_ood.pdf)). Peer vendors are registered (PDL since 2020; 6sense registration 563658).
- Publicly-available exclusion (§1798.140(v)(2)) covers government records and information the consumer made public, but does not reliably cover DI's derived "competitor activity" signals; do not rely on it to avoid "business"/broker status.

## 4. Legal-basis summary

| Flow | GDPR | CCPA/CPRA |
|---|---|---|
| DI compiles DI Data (DNS/SSO/cert discovery, named buyers, activity events) | DI = controller; Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interests (DI's and its customers'), documented LIA; Art. 14 notice (layered notice + first-contact disclosure, per Experian/CNIL); Art. 21(2) absolute objection; retention limits for behavioural signals | DI = business + data broker; collection of PI about consumers with no direct relationship; privacy policy with CCPA metrics; Delete Act registration + DROP |
| DI licenses DI Data to customer (API/MCP/Clay/CRM/Slack) | Controller-to-controller transfer between separate controllers (EDPB para 70); Module 1 SCCs + UK Addendum if DI exports from EU/UK or if DI-US receives from an EU customer; customer must have its own lawful basis for its use (typically Art. 6(1)(f) B2B prospecting, subject to ePrivacy/PECR for the channel) | "Sale" to a "third party"; §7053 contract terms; "Do Not Sell or Share" honoured by DI; customer must honour its own opt-outs |
| Customer uploads account/contact lists for matching | DI = processor under Art. 28(3) terms; no own purposes; no combining into DI Data; DSRs forwarded; Module 2 SCCs if EU customer → US DI | DI = service provider under §7051; no sale/share; no retention or use outside the direct relationship; no combining |
| Optional: customer opts in to let DI reuse uploads to improve DI Data | Would make DI an independent controller for that reuse (ZoomInfo S&I model); needs a separate, opt-in C2C clause and its own lawful basis | Would make the customer a seller/sharer to DI; avoid unless deliberately productised |

## 5. Risks of getting it wrong

1. **Calling DI a "processor" for DI Data.** The label does not stick (EDPB para 29; ICO), so DI would still carry controller duties (Art. 14 notices, LIA, DSRs, breach notification) while having told customers it has none — misrepresentation plus a §7051 contract that DI cannot honour (DI necessarily "sells" DI Data to many customers). Customers' own LIAs would be built on a false premise.
2. **Calling the whole relationship "controller-to-controller".** Then DI has no Art. 28 contract for uploaded lists; customers lose Art. 28 protection and DI's EU/UK customers cannot lawfully share their lists with DI. Belgian warning 15/2025 shows customers are being asked by regulators to prove vendor Art. 28 compliance.
3. **Letting uploads leak into DI Data without an opt-in clause.** DI becomes an unauthorised controller for that reuse (Art. 28(10); EDPB para 81) and breaches the mandatory §7051 "no combining" term.
4. **Not registering as a California data broker / not polling DROP.** $200 per day per lapse, plus $200 per unprocessed deletion request per day; CPPA is already fining small brokers. Also check Vermont, Texas and Oregon registries.
5. **Thin Art. 14 / LIA for the "competitor activity" signal.** The ICO's Experian findings and CNIL's reasonable-expectation test both bite harder on behavioural signals than on directory data. Without a layered notice, a first-contact disclosure path for customers, retention caps and a working objection/suppression list, legitimate interest is contestable.
6. **DSR routing errors.** Answering a data subject about a customer's uploaded list (processor flow) discloses that customer's data; refusing to answer about DI Data (controller flow) breaches Arts 12–21. The two tracks must be explicit in the procedure.
7. **Joint-controller language.** Avoid it; it creates Art. 26 arrangements and joint liability with every customer. No peer uses it.

## 6. Sentences Mo can use verbatim in the DPA preamble

1. "This DPA has two parts. Part A applies where Deal Intelligence processes Customer Data on Customer's behalf; Part B applies where Deal Intelligence makes DI Data available to Customer."
2. "With respect to Customer Data, Customer is the Controller and Deal Intelligence is a Processor acting solely on Customer's documented instructions."
3. "With respect to DI Data, Deal Intelligence and Customer are each separate and independent Controllers; nothing in this DPA creates a joint-controller relationship."
4. "Deal Intelligence compiles DI Data for its own purposes, under its own lawful basis, and is responsible for its own compliance with Data Protection Law, including transparency obligations and responding to data-subject requests concerning DI Data."
5. "Customer determines its own purposes and lawful basis for its use of DI Data and is responsible for its own compliance, including honouring objections to direct marketing."
6. "Deal Intelligence will not combine Customer Data with DI Data, or use Customer Data for any purpose other than providing the Services, unless Customer expressly opts in under a separate written addendum."
7. "For purposes of the CCPA, Deal Intelligence is a 'business' and a registered 'data broker' with respect to DI Data, Customer is a 'third party' when receiving DI Data, and Deal Intelligence is a 'service provider' with respect to Customer Data."
8. "Deal Intelligence relies on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) to compile and license DI Data, maintains a documented legitimate-interests assessment, and provides notice to data subjects in accordance with Art. 14 GDPR."
9. "Where Deal Intelligence receives a data-subject request relating to Customer Data, it will forward the request to Customer without undue delay and will not respond to the data subject except to redirect them to Customer."
10. "Where Deal Intelligence receives a data-subject request relating to DI Data, it will respond as Controller, suppress the individual from DI Data, and propagate the suppression to Customer through the Services."
